Native locomotion

What does an explorer learn when the unfamiliar world is its own body?

This experiment applies M2E directly to a simulated humanoid. The agent controls the body’s joints and senses its physical state. It receives no demonstrations, target gait, or reward for walking. The environment’s task reward is discarded; learning is driven by the novelty of bodily observations.

As familiar movements become less rewarding, the agent seeks new combinations of poses and velocities. Moving the body through space opens up more of those experiences. Locomotion develops because it is a productive way to explore.

The same exploration principle used to navigate a maze can therefore produce bodily control. The agent learns how to move as part of discovering what it can do, without a separate locomotion objective.
